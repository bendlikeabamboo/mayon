# Card 005 — Jump-to-setting search (wild card)

## Story

You press cmd-K on the settings page, type "backup", and land on the Backup section — scrolled into view and briefly highlighted. Sections are never reorganized at all; instant retrieval replaces browsing. The index is just the static list of section names and headings, so it is cheap to build and can't drift out of date. Your own words hint at this: "I was looking for backup" — you already knew its name.

## Playthrough (2026-08-28)

- **What & why (confirmed at deal time)**: What — reaching any specific settings area, like Backup, is quick and self-evident, without scrolling past everything else. Why — the settings page is one long scroll today; length plus the cognitive load of scanning every section makes finding a known section slow and frustrating.
- **How it goes**: An afternoon builds it — a small search field (plus a cmd-K binding) over a static index of section names, headings, and a few aliases ("restore", "api key"); a hit scrolls the long page to the section and flashes the heading. Your backup story plays out exactly: name known, target reached in seconds. Then reality: this app has no palette culture today, so nobody presses cmd-K unprompted — the search field must sit visibly at the top of settings, permanent new chrome, and the alias list becomes a small thing to keep honest forever.
- **Snags**: Affordance discovery — a hidden gesture is a feature nobody uses; a visible box is a promise that needs aliases maintained as sections are added or renamed. Zero help for browsing: search only serves known names, and the overload complaint lives partly in the "what's even in here" case. The alias table quietly re-imports taxonomy judgment — Card 003 in miniature. Mobile needs the visible field, not the gesture.
- **Trade-offs**: No structural change at all — the page stays exactly as long; the entire win is retrieval speed for known targets; a permanent index to maintain.
- **Delivers the what?**: Fully for the literal pain (backup reached in seconds, within days of work); partially for overload — it dissolves only for known-name visits and does nothing for discovery browsing.
